# Simbiosis Homes — Concept Overview

![CyberNest Montana](../assets/visualization-exterior.png)

## 1. The Problem

California attracted a generation of top engineers, designers, and AI specialists. It is now overcrowded, expensive, and politically strained. Meanwhile, across the United States, there are vast, beautiful, underpopulated regions with cold climates, cheap land, and no structural capacity to host the next wave of AI-driven work.

At the same time, every AI specialist faces the same three problems:

- **Cloud dependency** — compute is rented, not owned. Costs are unpredictable, latency is high, and data leaves the building.
- **Energy waste** — data centers burn up to 40% of their energy on cooling, dumping heat into the atmosphere while nearby homes burn fuel to stay warm.
- **Housing cost** — a modest condo in California costs more than a large, custom, energy-autonomous home in Montana.

## 2. The Idea

Simbiosis Homes is a residential building that **heats itself from the waste heat of an embedded AI compute cluster**, with a modular, swappable cooling spine, a tropical greenhouse, and full energy autonomy.

The building is not a data center with beds attached. It is a **home first** — designed for families, couples, or individual specialists — that happens to produce compute as a byproduct of keeping its residents warm.

Three configurations are planned:

- **Single-family home** — one owner, one compute spine.
- **Duplex** — two families, shared infrastructure, separate living units.
- **Multi-unit** — four or more apartments, shared compute spine, shared greenhouse, shared garage.

## 3. Why Cold Climates

Cold climates turn a problem into an asset.

- **Free cooling** — the outside air is a natural heat sink for most of the year.
- **Heat reuse** — every watt spent on computation becomes a watt of indoor heating.
- **No cooling towers** — no evaporative water loss, no industrial noise, no visual pollution.
- **Land availability** — Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, and similar regions have space, scenery, and low population density.

## 4. What the Building Does

- **Heats itself** from processor waste heat via a liquid cooling loop.
- **Cools itself** in summer by routing excess heat to the greenhouse or to ground storage.
- **Grows food** in a tropical greenhouse heated by the same loop.
- **Collects and reuses condensate** for irrigation and technical water.
- **Stores energy** from rooftop solar and a small wind turbine for night lighting and critical loads.
- **Sells excess compute** to external clients when the residents are asleep or not using the full capacity.
- **Runs locally** — no cloud dependency for the residents' own work.

## 5. Why It Matters for the State

- **Attracts top specialists** who want autonomy, privacy, and nature — without giving up compute power.
- **Creates local jobs** in construction, maintenance, and compute operations.
- **Brings tax revenue** from high-income remote workers and small compute businesses.
- **Uses existing land and grid infrastructure** more efficiently than a centralized data center.
- **Demonstrates a new model** for rural economic development in the AI era.

## 6. Why It Matters for Investors

- **Dual revenue** — housing value plus compute revenue.
- **Lower operating costs** — heating is a byproduct, cooling is free for most of the year.
- **Premium positioning** — a home that pays for itself is a new category.
- **Scalable model** — the same design can be replicated across cold-climate regions.

## 7. Why It Matters for Engineers

- **Modular cooling spine** — swappable compute modules, independent liquid loops, hot-swap without stopping the building.
- **Seismic-aware design** — flexible connections, isolated foundations, above-ground service channels.
- **Cold-climate engineering** — insulated shallow channels, condensate drainage, freeze protection.
- **Full redundancy** — dual communication routes, backup power, fail-safe heat routing.

## 8. What Exists Today

- **Qarnot** (France) — home radiators that compute and heat.
- **Cloud&Heat** (Germany) — server racks in residential basements.
- **Passive house standards** — ultra-low heating demand, but no compute integration.
- **Modular data centers** — but not designed as homes.

No one has combined all of these into a single, replicable, residential product designed from the ground up for cold climates. Simbiosis Homes is that product.
