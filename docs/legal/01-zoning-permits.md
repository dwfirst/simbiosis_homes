# Legal & Permitting — Zoning, Permits, and Water Rights

## 1. Purpose

This node defines the regulatory pathway for building the project on unincorporated county land in Montana. It covers the Land Use Permit (LUP), the state building permit exemption for fewer than five dwelling units, seismic design category implications, water rights under the exempt well provision, and the treatment of the greenhouse and accessory structures.

The reference county is Gallatin County (unincorporated). Other counties may differ. Confirm with the specific county Planning Department before design freeze.

## 2. State Building Permit: Exempt for Fewer Than Five Dwelling Units

Montana state law exempts **residential buildings containing fewer than five dwelling units** (single-family, duplex, triplex, fourplex) from state building permits in unincorporated areas, provided they do not serve transient guests [citation:3][citation:7]. Private garages and storage buildings used exclusively by the owner are also exempt [citation:7].

**For this project:** the 1–4 unit configuration falls under this exemption. The main house and the technical building are exempt from the state building permit, provided the technical building is not classified as a commercial enterprise.

**Base requirement:** if the configuration exceeds four dwelling units (e.g., a fifth unit is added), a state building permit is required. The design must not assume the exemption is permanent.

**Variant A:** if the county or state interprets the technical building as commercial (because compute is sold externally), the exemption may not apply to that building. In that case, a state building permit is required for the technical building only. Cost change: +$2,000–$5,000 in permit fees and plan review time.

## 3. County Land Use Permit (LUP): Required

Even where the state building permit is exempt, a **county Land Use Permit (LUP)** is required in most county zoning districts prior to construction [citation:3]. The LUP is the primary regulatory gate for the project.

- **Application:** site plan, project description, geotechnical report for steep or ridgeline properties, flood elevation certificate if in a floodplain [citation:3].
- **Review:** county staff reviews for zoning compliance. Processing time varies by complexity [citation:3].
- **Fees:** refer to the county fee schedule. State permit fees (if applicable) are approximately 1–2% of project valuation [citation:3].

**Base requirement:** the LUP application must be submitted and approved before any construction begins. The site plan must show the three buildings, the service channel, the parking area, and the setbacks.

**Variant B:** if the county does not have zoning (some unincorporated areas do not), the LUP may not be required. Confirm with the county clerk before proceeding.

## 4. Seismic Design Category

Most of western Montana is Seismic Design Category D under the IBC, though the IRC may assign a lower category for the same location because of different calculation methods [citation:6]. Bozeman design criteria explicitly list **Seismic Design Category D** [citation:13].

**For a 4-unit building:** the structure is not a single-family or two-family dwelling under the IRC. It falls under the IBC. A Montana-licensed structural engineer is required for the seismic design, regardless of the IRC exemption for smaller dwellings [citation:6].

**Base requirement:** the structural engineer must confirm the seismic design category using the 2021 IBC (or the code adopted by the jurisdiction at the time of permit). The 2024 IRC/IBC adoption is in progress but not universal as of 2026 [citation:3].

## 5. Water Rights: Exempt Well Provision

A groundwater well with a use of **35 gallons per minute or less, not exceeding 10 acre-feet per year**, is exempt from the full water right permit process [citation:5].

**Procedural requirements (effective 2026):**
- Submit **Notice of Intent to Appropriate Groundwater (Form 602I)** before putting water to use [citation:5].
- Driller submits **Well Log Report (Form 603)** within 60 days [citation:5].
- Submit **Notice of Completion (Form 602)** within 60 days after the development is put to use [citation:5].

**For this project:** the primary water source is condensate recovery and roof catchment (Node 06). A well, if drilled, is backup. This keeps the project within the exempt threshold and reduces regulatory burden.

**Base requirement:** if the project exceeds 35 gpm or 10 acre-feet per year (e.g., for large-scale greenhouse irrigation), a full water right permit is required. The design must not assume the exemption covers unlimited use.

**Variant C:** if the project is in a closed or monitored aquifer (e.g., Gallatin Valley), the exempt well provision may be restricted or subject to additional metering and reporting [citation:12]. Confirm the aquifer status before drilling.

## 6. Greenhouse: Accessory Structure or Permitted Building

In some Montana counties, **greenhouses for personal use only, with no public access**, are exempt from building permits [citation:2]. In Missoula County, greenhouses used only by the residential property owner are exempt, but if people other than the owner will be in the structure, it cannot be exempt [citation:9].

**For this project:** the greenhouse is part of the residential complex and is used by the residents (not the public). If the county treats it as an accessory structure to the residential use, it may be exempt. If the county treats it as a separate structure with public access (e.g., if produce is sold), a permit may be required.

**Base requirement:** the greenhouse must be classified as a residential accessory structure. No public access, no commercial sales from the greenhouse.

**Variant D:** if the residents want to sell surplus produce, the greenhouse becomes a commercial use. A building permit and possibly a conditional use permit may be required. Cost change: +$2,000–$5,000, plus ongoing compliance.

## 7. Wildfire-Urban Interface (WUI)

If the site is in a designated WUI zone, additional requirements apply:
- Propane tanks must be buried or protected (Node 07).
- Defensible space and fire-resistant construction may be required.
- WUI codes vary by county and are not uniformly implemented [citation:11].

**Base requirement:** confirm WUI status with the county fire marshal before finalizing the site plan. The propane tank placement (Node 07) and the roof materials depend on this.

## 8. Flexibility Clause

Every parameter in this document can be changed to reduce cost or to meet the client specific needs. However, the following must be preserved:

- The LUP must be obtained before construction.
- The dwelling unit count must remain below five to preserve the state building permit exemption.
- A licensed structural engineer must design the seismic system.
- Water use must remain within the exempt well threshold (35 gpm / 10 acre-feet).
- The greenhouse must remain a residential accessory, not a commercial operation.

## 9. Reverse Engineering: If Conditions Change

| Condition | Change | Cost Direction |
|---|---|---|
| County has no zoning | LUP not required | -$1,000–$3,000 |
| Technical building classified as commercial | State building permit required for that building | +$2,000–$5,000 |
| Site in closed aquifer | Exempt well may be restricted; metering required | +$1,000–$2,000 plus ongoing reporting |
| Greenhouse used for commercial sales | Building permit and conditional use permit required | +$2,000–$5,000 |
| Site in WUI zone | Buried propane, fire-resistant materials | +$3,000–$10,000 |
